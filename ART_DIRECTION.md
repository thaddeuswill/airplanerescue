# Art direction

Taken from the reference board: https://www.pinterest.com/thaddeuswilloughby/my-ap/
(sections: Environment, catalog, mechanic/airplane, hangar, Aircraft, Airport, game mechanics)

## The one-line pitch
**A traveling A&P mechanic in rural mid-century Americana.** Medium-poly, stylized-realistic, painterly. Think Firewatch's warm, layered landscapes mixed with a 1950s–70s tool catalog.

## Look
- **Geometry: medium poly.** Real silhouettes and bevels, but no tiny detail modeled in. Planes should read as real GA aircraft: high-wing trainers, bush planes on tundra tires, a low-wing tourer, a vintage biplane.
- **Textures: hand-painted or stylized-realistic, 512–1024 px.** Wear and grime are painted in (rust streaks, faded paint, oil stains, chipped edges) rather than coming from photos.
- **Light: golden hour by default.** Warm low sun, long shadows, hazy distance. Also dusk with sodium-orange streetlights, misty mornings, and overcast gray days.
- **Atmosphere:** strong fog and aerial perspective, with layered silhouettes of tree lines and hills fading into haze.

## Palette
- **Land:** golden wheat, dry prairie tan, deep pine green, red-dirt roads, gravel gray
- **Built:** barn red, weathered white clapboard, faded brick, galvanized and corrugated metal, rust orange
- **Accent (catalog and signage):** tool-box red, cream, mustard yellow, black, teal and green from 1960s aircraft brochures

## Places (Environment section)
Gravel and dirt roads, red barns, farm fields, pine forests, rivers and lakes, wooden docks, fire lookouts, power-line poles, water towers, small-town brick garages, 1950s gas stations with round-top pumps, roadside groceries, hand-painted wall signs and ghost-sign ads. Small rural airfields belong in this world, not big airports.

## Hangar and shop (mechanic/airplane, hangar)
Rolling toolboxes (red, yellow and blue, covered in stickers), pegboard tool walls, worn wood workbenches, bins of bolts and washers, parts manuals, oil cans, and hand-written service notes.

## Two layers: the 3D world and 2D work scenes
- **The 3D world is for looking, not reading.** You walk the field, take in the view and walk up to the part you want to work on. No floating labels or screen clutter, so the world stays beautiful while you work.
- **Life in the world:** grass and wheat swaying in the wind, trees rustling, birds crossing the sky and landing on fences and hangar roofs, a windsock moving with the wind, insects in the sun, dust drifting across gravel. **Sound** does a lot of the work: birdsong, wind in the grass, a distant tractor or dog, the creak of hangar doors.
- **Work happens in 2D scenes.** Press E at a spot (left wing root, engine left side, instrument panel, gear leg) and the view cuts to a flat, hand-painted close-up of that spot only. One fixed angle, no orbiting, and no full 3D engine. Each spot is its own illustrated scene, so detail goes where you look and nowhere else.
- **Work scenes are layered and hands-on.** Grime, then a dirty inspection panel held by screws. Click each screw out, lift the panel off, and the inside is revealed (turnbuckles, cables, a cracked pulley). Do the work by clicking and dragging: loosen, adjust, swap, then put the screws back and tighten them. An instrument swap goes: screws out, gauge slides out, new one in, screws in, tighten. It should feel like real work, one part at a time.
- **Work-scene look:** painted to look 3D (light, cast shadow, thickness, wear) but built as flat 2D layers. One warm key light, like a work lamp or the low sun, from the upper left. Grime, oil, rust and safety wire are painted in. Hardware is chunky enough to click easily. The parts you can interact with are lit a little brighter rather than outlined.
- **Feedback is physical:** screws pop out with a small bounce and roll into a magnetic tray, panels lift with a shadow, and there's a ratchet click and wrench sound for every action. No timing mini-games.

## HUD: almost none
- **No notes or labels stuck on the screen.** Drop the sticky-note HUD and the prompt text in the middle of the screen.
- Only a **tiny center dot** for aiming. When you look at something you can work on, it becomes a small icon (a wrench, an eye, a hand) with no text.
- **Money, day and job details are tucked away** in things you carry. A **wallet** (one key or a corner icon) opens to show cash and receipts. A **clipboard** holds the work order and owner's notes, and a **logbook** holds your career. Each opens as a 2D item in your hands, styled like the catalog and paper forms.
- Important events (low cash, the owner's deadline) arrive in the world: a radio crackle, an owner's note tucked into the clipboard, a phone ringing in the hangar.

## UI and print (catalog section)
The parts shop and menus should look like **vintage tool catalogs and ads**: halftone printing, bold slab and script lettering, red/cream/black layouts, price tags, stamped part numbers. Logbooks and inspection sheets look like aged paper forms with hand-written notes.

**Use made-up brands** in that style, never real logos (Snap-on, Craftsman, Cessna and others appear on the board only as style reference).
